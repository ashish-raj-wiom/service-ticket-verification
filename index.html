01

The problem

When a CSP taps Resolved, Wiom’s system already checks whether the customer’s internet is up. Then it closes the service task either way. One in three closures happens while the system check says the internet is down, and those customers come back 3.4 times as often.

14,390service tasks a month closed as “resolved, system verified” while the system check said the customer’s internet was down (33.9% of checks)
17.9% vs 5.3%reopen rate after closing on a “down” check vs an “up” check. About 1,600 avoidable reopens a month
0customers asked whether the fix worked. The code has no working path for it

Three things go wrong at once:

  • The check does not decide anything. The system check’s answer is fetched, stored and ignored. A “down” closes exactly like an “up”.
  • The CSP never learns. The CSP app hides the result once the card is resolved, and the Technician app leaves the screen before the result exists. The CSP’s quality score always records a verified fix.
  • The customer is told, not asked. They get a “resolved” message with no way to say “it is still not working”. Their only option is to call again.

The worst subtype is recharge done but no internet: the customer has paid and still has no connection, and 49.1% of those close while the check says down.

02

Guardrails

What the solution must respect, whatever its design. Set by the PM and checked against the Customer OS lean register in the Customer OS PRD kit. “Recovery” is the Recovery OS v3.0, “SQ” is SQ Driver v2.0, and C2 and C4 are rules of the Customer OS constitution. Every time limit is a parameter, listed below the table.

Words used here. Resolved: the CSP marks the ticket resolved. Verification: the state a ticket is in after Resolved, until Closure. Verdict: Wiom’s check of the customer’s internet after Resolved, passed (up) or failed (down or no data). Reopened: during Verification, the ticket goes back to the CSP. Closure: the ticket is closed, for good.

G2The CSP must mark the ticket Resolved before it can reach Closure. Resolved is a claim, never proof that the internet works.REC-01; SQ-16; Recovery §10.1

G5The customer’s answer counts only as a plain yes or no: a button, a key press, or an agent recording it. Tone and free text are not answers.REC-10
Closing a ticket
G6A ticket reaches Closure only when at least three of these four are true: the CSP marked it Resolved, the internet is up, the customer has been told, and the customer said yes or stayed silent while the internet stayed up. G9 is the one exception, pending the governors’ ruling.Recovery §5.3; rec:closed
G7If the customer says yes during Verification, the ticket reaches Closure immediately, whatever the verdict.Recovery §5.3; SQ-03
G8If the verdict is passed and the customer doesn’t answer, the ticket reaches Closure 48 hours after Resolved. Silence on its own is neither a yes nor a no.REC-11; CP-REC-12
G9If the verdict is failed (internet down or no data) and the customer doesn’t answer, the ticket reaches Closure 7 days after Resolved.Conflicts with REC-01, the rec:closed gate, SQ-11, C2
G10Every Closure records why it happened: the customer said yes; the customer didn’t answer for 48 hours with the verdict passed; or the customer didn’t answer for 7 days with the verdict failed.Product decision
G11The resolution signal goes to Quality only at Closure.Support Resolution OS (signal on CLOSED)
When the customer says it isn’t working
G12A ticket that has reached Closure is never Reopened.Nothing leaves rec:closed
G13If the customer says it isn’t working during Verification, the ticket is Reopened immediately, whatever the verdict. It goes back to the same CSP, who is told it is the same issue, not a new one. When the CSP marks it Resolved again, a new Verification starts.REC-11; Recovery §10.2
G14If the customer says it isn’t working after Closure, a new ticket opens.REC-12 (a repeat within 30 days is a failed fix); C2
Telling the customer
G15Wiom contacts the customer about the fix only once the CSP has marked the ticket Resolved and Wiom has a verdict. If the verdict is passed, the customer is told the issue is fixed and can say if it still isn’t working; their reply shows whether the underlying issue is really resolved. If the verdict is failed, the customer is asked whether the issue is resolved, because the router may simply be off while nobody is home, or Wiom’s data may be wrong.REC-09; REC-15; C4
G16A message counts as told only once it is delivered, not when it is sent. If delivery fails, Wiom tries another channel.REC-18; REC-23
The CSP’s TAT
G17The CSP’s TAT counts only the time the CSP spends on the ticket, inside the CSP’s working hours. Time spent verifying, by Wiom or the customer, never counts.CSP-side TAT, outside the Customer OS
G18When a ticket is Reopened, the CSP’s clock resumes where it stopped, and the final TAT adds up every round. Example: raised 11:00, Resolved 15:00 (4 hours), in Verification until 17:00 (not counted), Reopened 17:00, Resolved again 20:00 (3 hours). TAT: 7 hours.CSP-side TAT, outside the Customer OS

Backend flow

The flow at its simplest, drawn from the table above. The guardrails set what must hold at every step. A complaint after Closure is a new ticket.

flowchart TD
  A["CSP marks the ticket Resolved"] --> B{"Verdict: is the customer's internet up?"}
  B -- "Passed" --> P["Customer told it is fixed"]
  B -- "Failed or no data" --> F["Customer asked if it is fixed"]
  P --> PA{"Customer"}
  F --> FA{"Customer"}
  PA -- "Yes" --> C1(["Closure"])
  PA -- "No" --> R(["Reopened: back to the same CSP, same issue"])
  PA -- "No answer for 48 hours" --> C2(["Closure"])
  FA -- "Yes" --> C3(["Closure"])
  FA -- "No" --> R
  FA -- "No answer for 7 days" --> C4(["Closure"])
  R --> A
  C1 --> Q["Quality gets the resolution signal"]
  C2 --> Q
  C3 --> Q
  C4 --> Q
