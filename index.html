01

The problem

When a CSP taps Resolved, Wiom’s system already checks whether the customer’s internet is up. Then it closes the service task either way. One in three closures happens while the system check says the internet is down, and those customers come back 3.4 times as often.

14,390service tasks a month closed as “resolved, system verified” while the system check said the customer’s internet was down (33.9% of checks)
17.9% vs 5.3%reopen rate after closing on a “down” check vs an “up” check. About 1,600 avoidable reopens a month
0customers asked whether the fix worked. The code has no working path for it

Three things go wrong at once:

  • The check does not decide anything. The system check’s answer is fetched, stored and ignored. A “down” closes exactly like an “up”.
  • The CSP never learns. The CSP app hides the result once the card is resolved, and the Technician app leaves the screen before the result exists. The CSP’s quality score always records a verified fix.
  • The customer is told, not asked. They get a “resolved” message with no way to say “it is still not working”. Their only option is to call again.

The worst subtype is recharge done but no internet: the customer has paid and still has no connection, and 49.1% of those close while the check says down.

Guardrails

What the solution must respect, whatever its design. Most come from the Customer OS documents in the Customer OS PRD kit; G11 to G14 are product decisions; G9 comes from the CSP-side Quality OS. “Recovery” is the Recovery OS v3.0, “SQ” is SQ Driver v2.0, and C4 is the Customer OS constitution’s clarity-of-state rule.

IDThe solution mustSource
What counts as proof
G1Treat the CSP’s Resolved tap, or anyone’s closure, as a claim. It is never proof the internet works.Recovery INV-REC-01, §10.1 (a CSP entry ranks lowest); SQ INV-SQ-16
G2Close only when at least three of four proofs are in: work done, internet up, customer told, and the customer’s side (a yes, or silence while the internet stays up).Recovery §5.3; the rec:closed state in the lean register
G3Count “internet up” only on sustained evidence, never on one reading.SQ INV-SQ-09, INV-SQ-11
G4Decide by fixed rules only. No scoring models, predictions or machine-learnt judgements of whether a CSP’s claim is true.Recovery INV-REC-08
G5Accept only a structured answer from the customer: a button, a key press, or an agent recording a plain yes or no. Tone, sentiment and free text are never evidence.Recovery INV-REC-10
Telling and asking the customer
G6Judge “customer told” on delivery, never on sending, and track delivery separately. If delivery fails, try another channel.Recovery INV-REC-23, INV-REC-18, §5.3
G7Keep the customer informed while the ticket is open.Recovery INV-REC-09; C4
G8Treat silence as neither yes nor no. Silence counts toward closing only while the internet stays up.Recovery INV-REC-11, §5.3
The CSP’s working hours
G9Expect the CSP to act, and run the CSP’s TAT clock, only inside the CSP’s working hours. Today that is the SR OS working window, 11:00–21:00 IST every day with no holiday exclusion, which already governs the 4-hour resolution TAT (SR_RESOLUTION_SLA_WINDOW_STANDARD, P91).Quality OS v1.16.5 amendment §5.1a (SR OS v1.6.2)
Repeats
G10Treat the same problem coming back within 30 days of a closure (CP-REC-03) as a failed fix, not a fresh problem. It carries more urgency than a first-time problem. After 30 days, it is a new problem and is treated as one.Recovery INV-REC-12 (REC-12); lean register, Failed recovery re-entry
Reopen and the CSP’s TAT
G11When a ticket reopens because the customer says the issue is still open, tell the CSP plainly that it is the same ticket and the same issue coming back, not a new one.Product requirement; consistent with Recovery §10.2 (the case stays open)
G12Never let verification time count against the CSP’s TAT. The clock stops when the CSP marks the issue resolved, and stays stopped while the ticket waits in verification for the customer to confirm. The wait does not touch the CSP’s metrics.Product requirement; the CSP’s TAT feeds Quality OS, outside the Customer OS
G13When the customer says the issue is still open, the CSP’s clock resumes from where it stopped. When the ticket finally closes, the CSP’s TAT is the working time the CSP spent across every round. Example: raised 11:00, resolved 15:00 (4 h), in verification until 17:00 (not counted), reopened 17:00, resolved again 20:00 (3 h), confirmed the next day. The CSP’s TAT is 7 hours.Product requirement; the CSP’s TAT feeds Quality OS, outside the Customer OS
Closure reasons
G14Tag every closure with the reason it closed, so anyone can tell later how the fix was confirmed. Each path in the backend flow has its own reason: system verification said the internet is up; the customer confirmed the issue is resolved; or the customer gave no response while system verification said the internet is up.Product decision

04

What the calls found

Calls made 8–12 Oct 2026 on 20 tickets a CSP or technician marked resolved while the customer’s router was offline (no ping in the 15 minutes before the resolve and none in the 30 minutes after). The CSP was called first, and the customer in three cases. CSP and customer names are left out.

These were early closes, not fake fixes. In all 20 cases the router came back online after the close, a median of about 6 hours later. The CSP marked the ticket resolved first and fixed it afterwards.

14 of 20CSPs reached. 6 did not pick up
20 of 20routers came back online after the ticket was closed
~6 hmedian from “resolved” to the router’s first ping (range 1 to 25 hours). The customer had no internet for that time after being told it was fixed
0confirmed cases of the internet working while Wiom read it as down. One is still open

Is it about TAT? On these calls, only 2 of the 14 CSPs reached named time or rating as the reason. The two most common answers were that they believed it was fixed (5) and that nobody at the CSP could say who closed it or why (4). Twenty calls cannot settle the question. The calls point to more than one cause, and TAT pressure is one of them.

What it means for the solution

#FindingWhat the solution should take from it
1The harm is the hours between “resolved” and the real fix, about 6 at the median, while the customer has been told it is fixedKeep the ticket open until the line is up. Most of these would then close by themselves within the day, and the customer would hear only the truth meanwhile
2In every case the fix came hours after the CSP’s markDecision needed. If the CSP’s time counts from the mark whenever the line comes up later, an early close still costs nothing. Count it to when the line actually came up, unless the line is up within the check window
3CSPs closed because they had no other way to report a blockerGive the CSP a way to say why it can’t be fixed now (road works, area outage, home switched off) that holds the ticket instead of closing it
4CSPs trust their own portal or OLT, which is not the customer’s NetboxShow the CSP what Wiom sees on the customer’s line, on the same ticket
5Owners and technicians give different accounts of who closed the ticket and whyThe check applies whoever taps Resolved
6A “recharge done, no internet” case was an unrenewed ISP planCheck the ISP plan before sending anyone to the customer

9 of the 20 did, up to 12 Oct 2026. Some came back on the same ticket; others raised a new ticket on the same router.

05

Offline verification, until the system is built

A daily routine that verifies early closes by hand. It fixes customers now and collects the numbers the build needs, such as how often a “down” reading is wrong. It follows the same guardrails as above.

The daily routine

StepWhenWhat happens
1 · Build the list10:30The call sheet refreshes with yesterday’s and this morning’s tickets marked resolved while the router was offline, and still offline. Tickets resolved less than 2 hours ago are left out, because ping data runs about 2 hours behind. Your earlier Status and Remarks are kept
2 · Hold back a control group10:30A random quarter of the list is marked “do not call”. Comparing it with the called tickets shows whether calling changes repeat contacts
3 · Re-check the lineBefore each callIf the router has come back online since the list was built, record the time it came back and skip the call. Most early closes resolve themselves within the day
4 · Call the CSP11:00–21:00Ask what was done and why it was closed. No blame. If they say their portal shows it up, ask for a screenshot and a photo of the MAC on the Netbox label
5 · Call the customer11:00–21:00, 2 triesAsk one question: “Kya aapka internet abhi chal raha hai?” (Is your internet working now?) Record yes, no, or not reached. Never tell the customer it is fixed unless they say so
6 · Act on the answerSame daySee the table below
7 · RecordAfter each callSet one outcome code in Status and add notes in Remarks
8 · ReviewWeeklyCounts by outcome code, the share that were really not fixed, the share of wrong “down” readings, and customer reach

Effort and limits

  • Effort. The list has run at 75–100 tickets a day. After the control group and the re-check, that is about 50–70 calls a day: two callers. One person should take a fixed random 25–30 a day and keep the method the same.
  • The ticket is already closed. Offline work can only correct afterwards. It cannot hold the ticket open or change the CSP’s TAT and score.
  • Ping data runs about 2 hours behind. Always re-check before calling.
  • Netbox MACs are not in the data warehouse. Confirming a wrong “down” reading needs tech’s device records or a label photo.
  • The call sheet holds customer contact details. Keep it internal.
